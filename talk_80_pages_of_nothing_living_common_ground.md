# 80 Pages of Nothing: Living Common Ground for Agentic Teams

_Length: 30 minutes_

Developers rarely experience their work as delivering to a specification. We experience it as solving problems. Then AI made writing specifications cheap. Building an agentic orchestration system, we received 80 pages of generated specification: well-structured, confident, and empty. It did not address the questions we actually ran into while building. On another project, eight months of specification fed two sprints of AI-native development. Different projects, same pattern: every handoff between phases produced more waste than understanding.

This experience report is about what we did instead. We stopped handing over specifications and started ingesting conversations. Discussions, decisions, and open questions go into an LLM-maintained wiki that both people and agents read from. The same source directs the team's understanding and the agents' context, and it changes as we learn. Instead of asking AI to confirm what we meant, we ask it to show us where our shared understanding has gaps and contradictions.

The specification becomes a by-product of problem solving, not its input. Scope stays put while understanding grows. You'll leave with a practical way to build living common ground for a sociotechnical system where some of the team members are agents.
