# Directing Attention with Doubtful Intent

_Length: 30 or 45 minutes_

People can now do things by asking for it. Asking for a change in our codebase to fix a problem. Asking for adding a feature. Asking for an entire system to a specification. Doubtful intent is about asking for things you doubt. I doubt the fix without side effects. I doubt the feature fits unspoken expectations, and us remembering to speak of our expectations. I doubt the systems work in all relevant scenarios and perspectives.

Through observing work under influence of AI, I see what don't work: isolating spec, dev and test with a handoff. For that reason, I collect practices for virtuous loops and cross-functional specialist and generalist teams, and in this talk, I will share three that I have found useful:

- Living common ground: bring specification into the team’s conversations and writing, building a shared knowledge base with role-specific views, traceable changes, and a path from text to executable specification. We fail with walls of text.

- System properties: use AI to identify candidate properties, have the team assess which matter, and evaluate built systems against them. Mismatches reveal gaps between expectations and behavior. We fail with example design without stronger programmable expectations.

- Model reconciliation: compare AI-generated models of a system with the team’s expectations, then investigate the gaps.
  Each experiment generates something to examine, surfaces differences, and reconciles them with evidence and shared understanding. Together, they show how doubtful intent can help teams decide what to look at next. We fail with traditional ways of testing in this speed and scale.

We need practices for the opposite ends: common ground grows from what we intend, properties and model reconciliation from what we built. Neither belongs to a tester or a phase; both are things the whole team does, developers included. You'll leave with practices to try, and a quality engineering view of collaboration where doubt directs attention to where value is at risk.
