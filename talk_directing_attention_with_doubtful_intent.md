# Directing Attention with Doubtful Intent

_Length: 30 or 45 minutes_

People now get things done by asking for them: a fix to our codebase, a new feature, an entire system built to a specification. Doubtful intent is asking while doubting what comes back. I doubt the fix comes without side effects. I doubt the feature meets the expectations we never thought to say out loud. I doubt the system works across all the scenarios and perspectives that matter.

Watching work under the influence of AI, I see what doesn't work: separating specification, development and testing with handoffs between them. What I look for instead are virtuous loops in cross-functional teams of specialists and generalists. In this talk, I share three practices I have found useful:

- **Living common ground**: bring specification into the team's conversations and writing. Build a shared knowledge base with role-specific views, traceable changes, and a path from text to executable specification. _Instead of walls of text._
- **System properties**: use AI to identify candidate properties, have the team assess which matter, and evaluate the built system against them. Mismatches reveal gaps between expectations and behavior. _Instead of hand-picked examples alone._
- **Model reconciliation**: compare AI-generated models of the system with the team's expectations, and investigate the gaps. _Instead of traditional testing that cannot keep up with this speed and scale._

Each practice generates something to examine, surfaces differences, and reconciles them with evidence and shared understanding. Common ground grows from what we intend; properties and model reconciliation start from what we built. None of them belongs to a tester or a phase. They are things the whole team does, developers included.

You'll leave with practices to try, and a quality engineering view of collaboration where doubt directs attention to where value is at risk.
